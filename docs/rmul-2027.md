# RMUL 2027 V1.0.0 standalone authority

`rm_sim_gameplay::rmul_2027::Match` implements the single-round gameplay
rules of the supplied Chinese **RoboMaster 2027 机甲大师高校联盟赛比赛规则手册**,
V1.0.0 (2026-10-15). Source SHA-256:
`a8fd7b7f6ea5212b9e4dfa590b478827992598d91912ed671653c8bdd9727fd6`.
See [the 2027 rules reference](rules-2027.md) for the wider interpretation.
Citations below refer to this UL manual's section/table numbers; printed page
numbers are one less than PDF viewer page numbers. The supplied PDF remains
external material under DJI's notices.

The module has two distinct serialized identities:
`Format::ThreeVsThree` / `Ruleset::Rmul2027V1` and
`Format::Infantry` / `Ruleset::RmulInfantry2027V1`. Both mean **V1.0.0**;
`Match::validate` rejects mismatches. Neither uses the UC match engine, UC
control accumulation, adaptive armor, experience, assembly, bases, outposts,
Rune, remote deliveries, paid HP purchases, or paid respawn. `crate::Team` is the
only shared rule type. Role IDs supplied by callers are unique opaque identities;
the manual's Heavy 1, Infantry 3 and Sentry 7 remain roster roles, not mandatory
numeric values of the application IDs.

## API and caller boundary

`Match::new(format)` creates an idle round. `add_robot(id, team, role)` admits
one robot per role/team while idle; Infantry format admits only Infantry.
Incomplete practice rosters are allowed, including an empty authority for
inspection. `start()` begins immediately. `begin_countdown()` starts the five
seconds in UL §5.5; `advance(dt_ns)` carries any excess countdown time into
running time. `finish()` compares the current counters; `reset()` preserves
roster and selected Infantry profiles and clears round state. There is no
implicit preparation/self-test clock. The caller owns pause, administrative
preparation and BO2/BO3 series scheduling.

The public `phase`, `format`, `ruleset`, `robots`, `teams`, `zones`, `result`,
`elapsed_ns`, and `countdown_remaining_ns` support presentation. `remaining_ns()`
returns remaining running time. Robot records expose HP, max HP, identity,
allowance, heat in tenths, lock causes, weakness and power state. `Robot::stats()`
returns native UL table properties, including the power limit in watts.

Call `check_launch(id, large)` before validating a physical muzzle path, then
`fire(id, large, speed_m_s)` after successful physical validation. `large` means
42 mm; only Heavy can fire it. Invalid/nonfinite speed and unavailable launchers
make no changes. An accepted overspeed launch consumes a round and adds heat;
its penalty locks subsequent launches. `record_launch` is a trusted referee
observation for a shot physically detected despite an allowance or hardware
lock; it implements overfiring, third postmortem shots and permanent heat locks.
It must not replace the ordinary launch admission path.

`damage(attacker: Option<u32>, victim, large)` consumes a physically accepted
armor hit and returns **actual HP removed**. Unknown/absent attackers receive
no damage credit or lifesteal. Friendly hits are ignored. The physical caller
owns the strict normal-speed thresholds (>12 m/s for 17 mm, >8 m/s for 42 mm),
plate detection timing (50 ms, potentially 200 ms for 42 mm), and projectile
collision geometry (UL Table 3-7, §3.3.1.1). `collision(id)` applies ordinary
2 HP damage through immunity/defense; `penalty_damage(id, amount)` bypasses
those defenses. `set_robot_hp` is a privileged operator command and clamps HP
to the native maximum; reductions can record defeat, while revival is explicit.

`observe_supply(id, detected)` reports only own-supply presence;
`observe_zone(id, Zone, detected)` reports any of the three UL control areas.
Presence stays active until two seconds after **loss**, rather than expiring
while a robot remains on a card. Death, offline main controller and weakness
make zone observations ineligible. Both highlands also give defense, regardless
of their controller. Supply detection clears weakness and respawn invincibility
on a live online robot.

`observe_tunnel(id, side, card)` is an **entry event**, not a repeated occupancy
sample: card is 0 or 1, side must be the robot's own side. Report any unrelated
card entry with `observe_other_card`; supply/zone detections also cancel a pending
tunnel pair. Pairing different own tunnel cards within an inclusive three-second
window gives 60 seconds of 1.5× cooling. The caller identifies and locates cards;
this authority contains no CAD or inferred field coordinates.

`configure_infantry` selects the 3v3 chassis/launcher before countdown.
`set_heavy_installed` reports actual attachment/removal on a live online Heavy;
the caller verifies workbench location and physical assembly. One correctly
detected 42 mm launch upgrades the Heavy. `buy_ammo` is a supply purchase:
each batch costs 10 gold for ten small or one large round; Sentry cannot buy.
`observe_power(id, actual_w)` takes measured motor power; `chassis_enabled`
reports referee actuation eligibility. `observe_modules` supplies main-controller,
speed-module and armor/supercapacitor connectivity. `observe_launcher_link`
reports whether Heavy launcher loss is below 5%.

## Explicit interpretation and timing policies

- **Full-HP duel tie:** UL §4.4(4)–(5) is more specific than §4.4(3). When both
  finish at full HP, compare actual rounds launched and then earlier first
  launch, even if they previously took damage and healed. Otherwise tied
  positive HP compares later first HP-loss time. An undamaged robot's first
  loss is treated as later than every actual loss. No shots on either side is
  a tie. This is the adopted interpretation of the lifesteal interaction.
- **Damage and healing:** attack credit and lifesteal use actual opposing HP
  removed, clamped at remaining HP. Lifesteal is rounded to nearest whole HP,
  with half HP upward (`div_ceil(2)`), following §3.3.1.1. It does not trigger
  from collisions, penalties or friendly damage. Supply/offline fractional
  rates accumulate integer remainders rather than losing fractions every
  settlement. Administrative HP changes never give projectile credit.
- **Permanent heat boundary:** Figure 3-12 explicitly uses `Q1 ≥ Q2`, whereas
  prose uses `>`. The implementation follows the inclusive UL flowchart.
  Death resets heat and the ordinary heat latch, but permanent round locks
  survive death; reconnecting a module clears only that module's lock cause.
- **Heavy upgrade HP:** changing the maximum from 100 to 450 adds the 350 HP
  difference to current HP, preserving previously lost HP. The manual does not
  separately specify partial-HP conversion. Successful upgrade persists after
  detaching the module and through death; detachment still blocks launch.
- **Canonical settlements:** the authority stores exact nanosecond caller time
  and its last 100 ms referee settlement. Cooling, power, healing, respawn and
  zone progress settle on the round's 100 ms grid; connectivity penalties and
  control depletion settle at 500 ms / one-second boundaries. This extends the
  manual's specified 10 Hz and 2 Hz periods to otherwise unspecified
  continuous-rate sampling. Contact/eligibility inputs are sampled at settlement;
  sub-100 ms input changes may affect the next full sample. Off-grid death's
  first respawn progress is capped at time actually spent dead, so revival is
  never early and may be delayed to the next settlement. A 33% reduction means
  a 67% requirement, not two-thirds. Dead robots outside own supply stay weak
  after their 30-second invincibility expires.
- **Simultaneous events:** zone transitions settle before that timestamp's
  control drain; the first scoring boundary at capture may therefore count the
  new controller. Control deductions are applied to both teams before checking
  the result; simultaneous zero control is a draw. Income and catch-up rewards
  are settled before revival/healing at a boundary. Sequential external damage
  commands follow caller order. The authority stops at the first result and
  ignores excess elapsed time.
- **Suppression:** inactivity lasts from a detected launch until exactly four
  seconds later. Merely spending the last legal round is not overfiring. A
  separate suppression latch records actual prohibited shots and bad links;
  a live online Heavy with positive allowance and a good link clears it.
  A successfully launched final round can still hit while allowance is zero.

## Checkpoints, tests and limits

Serde covers the **whole** `Match`, including format/revision, fixed-point
remainders, contact grace, pending tunnel pair, launch/first-loss timestamps,
locks, power buffer, control progress, catch-up awards and respawn progress.
After decoding trusted state, call `validate()` before using it. Validation
checks structural invariants and competition identity, not authenticity of
caller-owned referee state. Public fields support adapters and presentation;
ordinary gameplay changes should use methods so defeat/timing bookkeeping stays
consistent. Infantry checkpoints reject 3v3 control/economy/zone/buff state.
Checkpoint continuation and unpartitioned/partitioned advancement have exact
state-equality tests. The module also contains a runnable minimal-duel doctest.

Remaining boundaries: physical armor speed/debounce, attachment geometry and
workbench legality, field-card geometry and card-entry identification, Sentry
controller/autonomy, communication transport and packet-loss measurement,
inspection/conduct/penalty issuance, preparation/self-test procedures, BO2/BO3
and §4.4.1 series resolution remain caller-owned or unimplemented. This is a
single-round gameplay authority, not an official full tournament controller or
a claim of validated RMUL CAD. No RMUC field package is admitted by this module.

## Selectable simulator practice

Both binaries accept `--competition rmul-2027` (3v3) and
`--competition rmul-infantry-2027` (步兵对抗赛). The default remains
`rmuc-2027`. The title menu's Competition field selects the same modes, including
remote joins. A remote client must select the host's competition; a mismatch is
rejected with the required flag. No season Cargo features or 2026 execution paths
are added. Welcome identifies the competition even in `--no-referee` practice,
so a different selected practice layout is rejected before presentation.
Network peers must use protocol **83**, whose checkpoint carries
both the public RMUL view and the matching complete authority.

For a two-player duel, start a headless host and connect two app clients:

```sh
just server --competition rmul-infantry-2027
just run --competition rmul-infantry-2027 --connect 127.0.0.1:7700 --team red
just run --competition rmul-infantry-2027 --connect 127.0.0.1:7700 --team blue
```

The host's referee page is at `http://127.0.0.1:7780` by default. Use **Start
match** after deployment; it begins the five-second countdown. A local embedded
session can expose the same page with `--http 127.0.0.1:7780`. Pause, finish,
reset and bounded HP editing use the existing host commands. Infantry starts
with 200 rounds; it cannot buy or replenish ammunition. In 3v3, income begins
one second into the running round, and Heavy/Infantry buy through ordinary
ammunition controls while at their own practice supply. Profile choices apply
only to 3v3 Infantry and are fixed at countdown. Heavy's existing **J → Convert
to Hero** action is a virtual workbench attachment: stop inside own practice
supply, attach, buy a shell, and fire to confirm the native upgrade. The physical
Hero model remains a practice approximation; it does not become an RMUC Hero
roster entry. Reset retains the physical body proxy and clears attachment/round
authority, requiring installation again.

3v3 admits one Heavy 1 (`--robot engineer`), Infantry 3 and Sentry 7 per side.
Manual Sentry pilot admission is rejected because UL §3.1.3 requires autonomous
operation and prohibits multi-robot communication. Trusted operator Sentry
spawning retains the native Sentry properties, but autonomous firing is not
implemented. Infantry mode admits only one Infantry 3 per side. The host also
checks these rosters in `--no-referee` physical practice, and refuses duplicate
team slots instead of returning an unaddressed pilot. There is no AI Infantry
opponent; use another client for a duel.

No verified RMUL CAD package was supplied. These modes load **no RMUC CAD**, and
explicit `--cad-assets` overrides are rejected. The practice worlds have flat
floor planes, perimeter walls and nominal 12 × 8 m / 5 × 5 m extents from UL
§§3.2.1/4.2. They omit official highland decks, tunnel clearances, workbench
hardware and Infantry cover. Flat colored rectangles expose the 3v3 practice
antenna approximations below; these coordinates are simulator choices, not
measurements or dimensions asserted from the manual drawings. Coordinates are
world FLU metres, with red on +x; rectangle edges are inclusive.

| Practice card | Center x/y, m | Half extent x/y, m |
|---|---|---|
| Red supply / virtual workbench | +4.75, +2.75 | 1.10, 1.00 |
| Blue supply / virtual workbench | −4.75, −2.75 | 1.10, 1.00 |
| Red highland control/defense | +2.30, +0.80 | 0.65, 0.65 |

| Center control | 0, 0 | 1.30, 1.30 |

The adapter accepts chassis-center heights −0.10 through +0.50 m for these flat
cards, reports presence every explicit world tick and lets native authority
apply eligibility and two-second loss grace. The native tunnel-pair API is tested
but has no physical practice-card adapter, since no tunnel layout is fitted.
Measured wheel motor power feeds the native referee buffer. Module-fault
observations and installation commands remain host-authorized inputs.

Practice sessions currently advertise no client prediction scene. Driving and
projectile presentation therefore follow host updates; the normal local replay
and collision-debug geometry are unavailable until a practice-geometry contract
is provided. This does not change host physics or checkpoint restoration.

`FieldConfig.rmul_2027` selects the trusted in-process format. It is mutually
exclusive with `FieldConfig.rmuc_2027`. World contact scoring uses the shared
physical armor envelopes as approximate detectors, strict normal impact speeds
and 50/200 ms per-module detection intervals, then dispatches damage only to
RMUL authority. `Field::restore` rejects mixed competitions, different public and
hidden authorities, mismatched chassis rosters and RMUC assembly payloads. The
standalone rule tests, world contact/supply/capture tests, host admission tests,
app selection/HUD tests and checkpoint codec tests cover these boundaries.
